This instance asks for the two-term target

A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3+X_0^3\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1^2

on the central holdout sextic. A bundle can sometimes admit a compact construction even when no convenient formula for its constituent monomials is apparent. The point is to test that possibility without mistaking a specially chosen combination for unrestricted target coverage.

There is a particularly useful distinction here. The first summand is the central unresolved target. The second has nonconstructive support from a finite quotient: its residue is invariant under an automorphism whose quotient is rationally connected. The character calculation includes the volume determinant, not just the monomial's eigenvalue. Chapter 3 explains the finite-quotient support principle.

The two summands can also be separated using a diagonal symmetry of this exact F. Fix the X coordinates and send (Y_0,Y_1,Y_2) to (\zeta^2Y_0,\zeta^4Y_1,\zeta^2Y_2), with \zeta^6=1. This order-three action fixes the first monomial and multiplies the second by \zeta^4. Averaging the transformed rational-field identities therefore isolates the central target. If intermediate coefficients lie in a number field, taking the appropriate field trace returns a rational certificate.

Consequently, this is a strategic alternative presentation of the holdout problem, not another independent remaining direction. A construction for the bundle would still constitute real progress because its symmetry projection reaches the central class. Conversely, the known support of the companion does not already solve the bundle.

This is useful when choosing an ansatz: a target combination may align with a contact determinant, Jacobian density, or deformation formula more naturally than either summand alone. The challenge keeps that combination explicit, and its relation to the central target can then be checked rather than inferred from a suggestive resemblance.
