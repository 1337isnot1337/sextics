The target is the simple monomial \(X_0\cdot X_1\cdot X_2^3\cdot Y_1\), but the \(X\)-part of the sextic has many mixed terms. This is an intentional mismatch between a simple requested class and a less transparent equation. It asks whether the structure behind a Hesse construction can still be recognized without changing the target into a more convenient polynomial.

Useful quadratic quantities are
\[
u=X_0^2,\qquad v=X_2^2-2\cdot X_0^2,\qquad
w=X_1^2+X_0^2.
\]
They are independent as linear functions of the three coordinate squares. A cubic expressed in \(u,v,w\) can therefore look densely coupled in the original variables while retaining special plane-cubic geometry. The passage to those quantities is nonlinear in the six original coordinates, so it requires more care than an ordinary linear relabeling of a vector field.

The target has odd exponents in three \(X\) coordinates and one \(Y\) coordinate. The passive \(Y_1\) factor and the cubic power of \(X_2\) also constrain which adapted-coordinate densities can represent the requested class.

The instance comes from a construction family that was studied with the target held fixed. It is useful for testing recognition: can exact structure be recovered from an expanded equation, and can alternative polar presentations be understood as consequences of that structure?
