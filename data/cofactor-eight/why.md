The expanded sextic has many mixed monomials, but it is organized by a quadratic form \(C\) and a cubic form \(r\). Their principal structure is
\[
F=H(C,X_1,X_2)+4\cdot r^2+K(Y_1,Y_2),
\]
where \(H=C^3+C\cdot X_1^2\cdot X_2^2+X_1^6+X_2^6\). The forms \(C,r\) share \(X_0,Y_0\) and also involve other \(X\) coordinates, so this is a nonlinear organization of the equation rather than a visible partition into independent coordinate blocks.

The target has a matching density factor:
\[
A=J\cdot C\cdot Y_1,\qquad
J=C_{X_0}\cdot r_{Y_0}-C_{Y_0}\cdot r_{X_0}.
\]
The determinant \(J\) is the Jacobian of the active quadratic–cubic change. Its appearance is the substantive feature of the long target polynomial. A method that works after changing coordinates must transport the residue density as well as the equation; omitting \(J\) would ask for a different class.

This entry was selected while extending a cofactor construction beyond a single binary pair. Mixing additional even coordinates into \(C,r\) tests whether the method depends on their original diagonal form or only on their degrees, parity, and Jacobian. The passive binary sextic \(K\) adds another controlled source of non-diagonal terms.

The selected target is one direction in a natural eight-dimensional packet, obtained by varying a quadratic factor and a passive odd linear factor. The instance remains one fixed tuple, but that larger space explains why it is useful: a structural answer can potentially organize several classes at once.
