With \(p=X_2\cdot Y_2\), this holdout target is
\[
A=X_1^2\cdot p\cdot Y_0^2.
\]
It distributes its degree across all three coordinate pairs but remains linear in the endpoint product. This places it between targets concentrated in one pair and [the monomial using all six variables](#case/holdout-all-six). Its relatively simple expression in paired invariants is a reason to investigate quotient coordinates carefully.

The holdout is invariant under simultaneous sign changes within each pair. It also depends on the endpoint through the symmetric quantities \(X_2^6+Y_2^6\) and \(p\). These features suggest separating the first two projective ratios from the remaining surface geometry. In that description, however, the target's density must be transported together with the equation. Simply replacing \(X_2,Y_2\) by their product does not preserve the residue form without correction.

Exact support calculations identify this monomial as one of ten independent remaining directions in the paired-sign-invariant sector. The other 48 directions in that sector have geometric existence proofs from finite quotients. In particular, the endpoint reflection does not settle this target: its numerator is invariant, whereas the volume changes sign. This is a remaining class on the same variety, not a newly chosen sextic assumed to be generic.

The distribution \(X_1^2\cdot Y_0^2\) is the distinguishing feature. It couples a middle even coordinate to the initial odd coordinate while avoiding their partners. A candidate family of supports can therefore be tested against it separately from the cubic targets. Success would add an independent direction to the existing coverage; it would not automatically resolve all ten. Conversely, a failure in one denominator space would leave open geometric constructions using a different support or a more complicated relative primitive.
