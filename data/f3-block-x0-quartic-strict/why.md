Adding the single term \(X_0^2\cdot X_1^2\cdot Y_0^2\) to Fermat creates a Hesse cubic in three squared coordinates:
\[
p(u,w,x)=u^3+w^3+x^3+u\cdot w\cdot x,
\qquad (u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2).
\]
This is a small step beyond a binary coupling, but it introduces genuine plane-cubic geometry. Three active coordinates can no longer be treated as three independent sixth powers.

The target is the particular combination
\[
X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3-\frac53\cdot X_0\cdot X_1^4\cdot Y_0.
\]
Both terms belong to the same odd-coordinate character. Their relative coefficient records a direction in a two-dimensional target space, so distinguishing the bundle from its individual summands is essential. Independent constructions for different directions can be combined, but one bundle alone does not justify that conclusion.

The Hesse presentation offers two related kinds of geometry: tangent lines with a prescribed residual intersection, and conics along which the cubic acquires square contact. This instance provides a rational setting in which to compare those descriptions. It also makes visible a broader issue: a field can have several useful presentations, and the smallest pole need not be the one that first revealed the underlying mechanism.

The early calculations on this sextic helped organize the passage from isolated coefficient solutions to families of contact constructions. Its role in the atlas is therefore a foundational ternary-block example with an explicit target ratio. [The Hesse-web instance](#case/family-hesse-web) varies the cubic parameter and asks for a single monomial instead. Comparing the two clarifies which features belong to the chosen fiber, which to the target direction, and which persist throughout the pencil.
