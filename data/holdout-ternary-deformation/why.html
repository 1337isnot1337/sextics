This instance keeps the cover and trace of the central holdout, while changing the geometry on which its polynomial integration depends. The new terms are

\frac1{14}\bigl(X_0^5\cdot X_1+2\cdot X_1^5\cdot X_2+X_2^5\cdot X_0\bigr),

and the target remains A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3. Writing the modified ternary sextic as f(X), the Fermat-curve cover still gives the weighted quotient

H=f(X)+P\cdot(\alpha\cdot X_0^3\cdot X_1+\beta\cdot X_1^3\cdot X_2)+S^3-3\cdot S\cdot P^2, \qquad \alpha^6+\beta^6=1.

The interchange y\leftrightarrow T, its density factor, and the nontrivial deck character used to recover the target all persist. The exact conic used for the central equation does not: substituting it in the added ternary terms gives a nonzero polynomial. Reusing its coefficient matrix or primitive without constructing a replacement family would leave a gap.

Nonconstructive existence is known, by the cover-and-trace support argument in Chapter 4, using the quotient-support principles of Chapter 3. Over the generic point of the Fermat curve, H=0 is a quasismooth degree-six hypersurface in \mathbb P(1,1,1,2,2), with anticanonical class \mathcal O(1). Quasismoothness can be checked at (\alpha,\beta)=(1,0) and hence holds generically. Its three ambient quotient points have type \frac12(1,1,1). The resulting Fano threefolds are uniruled, and the cover's quotient fourfold has divisor-supported fourth cohomology. The same nontrivial deck character then isolates the prescribed class downstairs.

The challenge is to realize that support as an explicit rational-field certificate, or to formalize the geometric existence argument. A concrete starting point is the same finite-dimensional conic-incidence problem used for the central equation: find a conic with a nonsingular coefficient Jacobian, then prove that its algebraic family reaches the generic point. The asymmetric ternary perturbation removes the special parity blocks that simplified the original seed calculation.

Unlike the moving-branch companion, this equation does not force a new coefficient curve or a new trace projection. It isolates the question of how effectively the geometric family itself can be constructed. No denominator-degree bound or expanded witness is required by the formal challenge.
