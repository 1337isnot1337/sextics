This example replaces the triangle of a singular Hesse cubic by a line times a nondegenerate conic. Set
\[
U=X_0^2+Y_0^2,\quad V=X_0^2-Y_0^2,\quad
Q=\frac{101}{103}\cdot(X_1^2+Y_1^2).
\]
The structured part of the sextic is \(U\cdot(U^2+V^2+Q^2)\); the remaining terms are weighted spectator sixth powers. The conic is therefore visible in adapted quadratic quantities even though the original equation contains several couplings.

The target
\[
A=8\cdot X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot(X_0^2-Y_0^2)\cdot X_1^2
\]
contains the density of the \((X_0,Y_0)\mapsto(U,V)\) change. Its additional \(V\) and \(X_1^2\) factors select a particular direction compatible with the weighted structure. The nonzero scalar is a normalization; the placement of those factors is the substantive information.

A conic admits infinitesimal motions preserving its quadratic form. The question is whether such a motion can be combined with radial behavior in the remaining coordinates so that the original-coordinate divergence is correct. Preserving a polynomial is only half of that task. The trace of the linear action and the Jacobian density of transport determine whether the proposed field has the needed cancellation.

This family was developed by deforming the more degenerate Hesse triangle while keeping the target problem under control. It therefore tests a real enlargement of the auxiliary geometry, rather than merely rewriting the same factorization. [The Hesse triangle](#case/hesse-triangle) is the useful comparison point, and [Chapter 2](#technical-information/explicit-constructions) develops the transport framework. The challenge is to understand which features persist when three linear factors become a line and an irreducible conic, and which target restrictions remain essential.
