This sextic combines a Hesse block in X_0,X_2,Y_2, an unequal binary block in X_1,Y_1, and the remaining sixth power. The active-looking part is

X_0^6+X_2^6+Y_2^6+X_0^2\cdot X_2^2\cdot Y_2^2,

but the target X_0\cdot X_1\cdot X_2^3\cdot Y_1 also contains the two binary-block coordinates. This makes it possible to distinguish dependence of the target on a variable from a need for the field to differentiate that variable.

The non-diagonal binary coefficients 5,21,30,14 are retained deliberately. Replacing that block by two sixth powers would make the example easier to misread as an essentially diagonal one. The target's factor X_1\cdot Y_1 can potentially be passive even though the polynomial in those same variables is not diagonal; that assertion must be checked against the actual active field.

The Hesse pencil supplies special factorizations in the quadratic quantities X_0^2,X_2^2,Y_2^2. Such factorizations suggest several related polar divisors, potentially defined over different coefficient fields. The question is how they compare as presentations of the same requested class, and whether a compact rational representative can expose structure hidden in a larger coefficient search.

This instance was used as a control with its target fixed in advance, rather than as a claim of new all-target coverage. Its value is the clean separation between active contact geometry and a nontrivial passive block. Comparing it with the fixed-target Hesse example tests what happens when the quadratic coordinates themselves are mixed. The general cofactor and contact principles are developed in Chapter 2.
