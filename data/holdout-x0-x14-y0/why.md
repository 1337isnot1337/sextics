This holdout direction is
\[
A=X_0\cdot X_1^4\cdot Y_0.
\]
It puts four powers in the middle even coordinate and uses the initial pair only through \(X_0\cdot Y_0\). Such a distribution is familiar from binary and ternary contact constructions, where an active pair is multiplied by a polynomial in other coordinates. The issue here is whether that intuition survives the holdout's two coupled links.

The factor \(X_1^4\) cannot simply be declared passive. The sextic contains \(X_0^3\cdot X_1\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1\) and \(X_1^3\cdot X_2\cdot Y_1\cdot Y_2\), so motion in the initial pair interacts with \(X_1\), and motion in the middle pair sees both ends. A successful field must account for those interactions in its derivative and divergence terms simultaneously.

The target belongs to the four-dimensional remaining character component conjugate to the six containing the central cubic. These are components of the same rational Hodge substructure, but that relation does not supply a direct substitution between their polynomial representatives. Exact quotient calculations show that this class survives modulo the known supported space. Thus neither its paired-sign invariance nor its small number of variables makes it an already solved direction.

This entry offers a controlled comparison with [\(X_0\cdot Y_0^3\cdot Y_1^2\)](#case/holdout-x0-y03-y12), which occupies the same character component but concentrates degree in odd coordinates, and with [\(X_0^4\cdot X_2\cdot Y_2\)](#case/holdout-x04-p), which moves the quartic power to the chain's beginning. Finding one support that distinguishes or jointly serves these directions would reveal more than an isolated numerical fit. The general contact methods in [Chapter 2](#technical-information/explicit-constructions) provide useful comparisons without yet resolving this coupled case.
