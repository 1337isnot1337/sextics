This holdout target is
\[
A=X_0\cdot X_1^4\cdot Y_0.
\]
It places four degrees in the middle even coordinate and uses the initial pair only through \(X_0\cdot Y_0\). The shape resembles binary and ternary contact constructions with a polynomial multiplier. Here the multiplier is part of the coupled geometry, rather than an independent passive variable.

Indeed, \(X_1\) appears to the first power in the initial coupling and to the third power in the endpoint coupling. A field moving in the initial pair encounters those coefficients; a field moving in the middle pair sees both ends. The derivative and divergence identities must account for these interactions together. The numerator's three-variable support does not reduce the problem to a three-variable equation.

This target belongs to the four-dimensional character component conjugate to the six containing the central cubic, among the ten paired-sign directions missed by the direct quotient-support span. In particular, the endpoint reflection fixes its numerator but reverses the residue volume. Those quotient limitations explain the role of an auxiliary cover, without suggesting that the target remains beyond the successful construction.

On the cover
\[
T^6=Y_0^6+Y_2^6,\qquad
\alpha=Y_0/T,\quad\beta=Y_2/T,
\]
set \(S=Y_1^2+T^2\), \(P=Y_1\cdot T\). The source on the weighted quotient is particularly simple:
\[
B=\frac{X_0\cdot X_1^4}{2\cdot\alpha^4}.
\]
It is independent of both \(S\) and \(P\), although the quotient equation and its moving-conic primitive still involve those variables. The lifted correction has deck exponent \(-2\), so its normalized trace vanishes and the primitive returns exactly \(A\).

Compare [\(X_0\cdot Y_0^3\cdot Y_1^2\)](#case/holdout-x0-y03-y12), whose source contains \(P^2\), and [\(X_0^4\cdot X_2\cdot Y_2\)](#case/holdout-x04-p), whose source is also independent of \(S,P\). All three use the same trace character and conic geometry, but their polynomial source identities differ. They show how the common construction absorbs changes in exponent distribution without pretending that the corresponding coordinates are passive in the original equation.

The mechanism in [Chapter 2](#technical-information/explicit-constructions) requires more than integrating a polynomial in isolation: its normal derivative and the cover Jacobian must also match the protected target. This instance packages that requirement into a simple source, making it a useful companion to the sources with substantial \(S,P\)-dependence.
