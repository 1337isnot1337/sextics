This is a ternary-cubic example in the squared variables (u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2), with three remaining sixth powers as spectators. The distinguished line is \ell=x-u-w. Restricting the active cubic to \ell=0 makes its three intersections coalesce into an inflectional contact on the coordinate line u=0.

The target lies in the character

X_1\cdot Y_0\cdot\mathbb Q[u,w,x]_2.

In particular, its X_0 exponent is even. That choice ties the relevant coordinate line to the position of the contact, rather than merely selecting a second numerator on an otherwise identical problem. The square-coordinate pullback is ramified along the coordinate lines; moving the collision between them changes which odd-coordinate character the geometry can serve.

This entry belongs to the same configuration family as the first-character collision, but uses a different placement and target direction. Their comparison tests whether the contact criterion survives a change of character, and whether the relation between a plane-cubic line and an original-coordinate pole is intrinsic to the construction rather than accidental at one fiber.

A collision is also a useful boundary of the ordinary tangent-and-residual situation: the residual point is no longer distinct from the tangency point. A method intended to work in families must account for that degeneration instead of simply dividing by a quantity that vanishes there. The concrete rational coefficients make the boundary directly accessible to exact calculation. The problem remains the full key equation for this fixed target; neither an appealing contact picture nor the existence of a line on the cubic is, by itself, a certificate.
