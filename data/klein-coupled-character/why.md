The cyclic Klein block retains a useful order-seven action even after the mixed terms couple it to all three \(Y\) variables. For a primitive seventh root \(\zeta\), use weights \((1,2,4,0,0,0)\):

\[
(X_0,X_1,X_2,Y)\longmapsto(\zeta\cdot X_0,\zeta^2\cdot X_1,\zeta^4\cdot X_2,Y).
\]

Every term of \(F\) has weight zero. The residue volume also has weight zero, since \(1+2+4=7\). Our target \(A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\), however, has character \(\zeta^3\).

This distinction is the point of the example. [Chapter 4, §4.3](#technical-information/known-locus) describes a support mechanism for the invariant sector of the coupled Klein family, including nineteen \((3,1)\) directions. It does not include the character selected here. Averaging over the order-seven group kills this target instead of solving it. The uncoupled Klein examples in the atlas also have block decompositions and additional symmetry that are lost under these mixed terms.

The exact Hessian symmetrizer calculation shows that the coupled equation admits no nontrivial linear block splitting. Its projective diagonal symmetry group has order fourteen, generated by the displayed order-seven action and the global sign involution. Among these symmetries, no target-fixing element supplies a junior quotient singularity for the usual quotient-support argument. These are concrete exclusions of specified mechanisms, not a proof that every geometric approach fails.

There is nevertheless strong structure available for computation. Over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), numerator and denominator searches can be organized by the order-seven characters. If a rational certificate is found over an extension, the trace and equivariant normalization machinery may reduce the result back to rational data. Alternatively, a nontrivial correspondence might transfer support between characters without averaging them away.

In characteristic five the mixed coefficients disappear. The three Klein partials become \(X_2^5,X_0^5,X_1^5\), while the other three become \(Y_0^5,Y_1^5,Y_2^5\). They have no common projective zero, proving smoothness in characteristic zero. No existence theorem for this nontrivial character is being claimed by the entry.
