Put p=X_2\cdot Y_2. The target

A=X_0^4\cdot p

connects the beginning and end of the holdout chain while omitting both middle coordinates. It is a particularly simple way to ask whether a class spanning the two ends can be treated without placing the middle coupling explicitly in its numerator.

The absence of X_1,Y_1 from A does not remove them from the problem. They mediate both mixed terms of the sextic. A construction that differentiates only the visible target variables still encounters coefficients involving the middle pair, while a construction that moves in the middle pair must control how its two interactions combine. This distinction between numerator support and active geometry is central to the example.

The endpoint product is invariant under the reflection exchanging X_2,Y_2. Consequently, the residue has the opposite sign from the reflection-supported sector. More extensive quotient arguments likewise leave this target outside the known support. It is one of ten independent remaining paired-sign directions, and belongs to the four-dimensional character component conjugate to the six containing the central cubic.

The monomial is worth comparing with X_1^2\cdot p\cdot Y_0^2. Both are linear in the same endpoint product, but their other four degrees are distributed differently and their residue characters differ. A proposed endpoint-based construction therefore has to explain which parameter-dependent coefficients it can carry, rather than merely observing that it uses p.

This target can also test geometric approaches through the holdout's surface fibration: the endpoint is naturally part of the fiber description, whereas X_0 contributes dependence on the first projective ratio. Producing a globally homogeneous primitive is the substantive step beyond solving a relation on one selected fiber.
