Put p=X_2\cdot Y_2. The target

A=X_0^4\cdot p

connects the beginning and end of the holdout chain while omitting both middle coordinates. This separates the variables visible in the numerator from the variables that mediate the equation's two couplings.

The absence of X_1,Y_1 from A does not remove their role. A field differentiating the visible target variables still encounters coefficients involving the middle pair, while a field moving in that pair has to control its interactions with both ends. A primitive on one selected fiber of the ratio fibration would not, by itself, provide the globally homogeneous field required by the certificate equation.

The endpoint product is fixed by X_2\leftrightarrow Y_2, so the residue acquires the opposite sign from the sector supported by that reflection quotient. The wider direct quotient calculation also leaves the target outside its supported span. It belongs to the four-dimensional character component conjugate to the six containing the central cubic, within the ten paired-sign directions distinguished by that calculation. These are precise limitations of the original-variety quotient methods.

The auxiliary Fermat cover changes the useful symmetry. Write

T^6=Y_0^6+Y_2^6,\qquad \alpha=Y_0/T,\quad\beta=Y_2/T,\qquad S=Y_1^2+T^2,\quad P=Y_1\cdot T.

The covered equation admits Y_1\leftrightarrow T, and its weighted quotient has source

B=\frac{X_0^4\cdot X_2\cdot\beta}{2\cdot\alpha^5}.

This source has no S,P-dependence. The moving-conic construction nevertheless retains the complete quotient equation, including the middle couplings. The lifted correction has deck exponent -2; normalized trace kills it and recovers exactly the prescribed target.

A useful comparison is X_1^2\cdot p\cdot Y_0^2. Both targets are linear in the endpoint product, but their remaining degrees give different residue characters. The companion uses an S-dependent source and exponent -4, while this one uses a source in the even coordinates and exponent -2. The same conic family serves both, with the distinction recorded in the source and trace identities rather than in a new geometric construction.

Chapter 2 develops this source-independent machinery. Together these entries explain why transporting a certificate across targets requires an exact density calculation, and why the successful cover is more flexible than a method restricted to the endpoint product alone.
