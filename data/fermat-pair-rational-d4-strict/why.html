The Fermat sextic is the reference point for this challenge. Its six separate sixth powers make the relationship between homogeneity, rational poles, and the divergence term unusually accessible. The target

A=X_0^3\cdot X_1\cdot X_2\cdot Y_0

provides a compact starting point: one coordinate has exponent three, three others occur linearly, and two do not occur in the target at all.

The Fermat case already has a general treatment in Diamond's paper, and the subset-Euler construction in Chapter 2 provides an elementary route for this target. This entry therefore asks a more informative question than bare existence: how much of the six-variable geometry is actually needed, and how economically can its rational primitive be expressed?

The exponents distinguish a pair of active coordinates, X_0,X_1, from the multiplier X_2\cdot Y_0. The latter supplies the required odd parity without forcing the field to differentiate the Y block. At the same time, the binary sum X_0^6+X_1^6 has rational quadratic and quartic factors. That juxtaposition makes this a useful setting for comparing a denominator obtained from a whole sixth-power sum with one adapted to its factorization.

The example is a control for the coupled cases elsewhere in the atlas. It separates difficulties already present in the rational-field equation from those introduced by mixed monomials in F. Its role is to make a small construction intelligible and reusable; neither the title nor the target imposes a preferred denominator degree on the underlying problem.
