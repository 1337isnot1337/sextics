A binary cubic can be organized around one of its simple roots. After choosing independent linear forms R,S in the active squared coordinates, it takes the shape

p=\alpha\cdot R^3+\kappa\cdot R\cdot S^2.

This separates a cube from a term with a repeated factor. It suggests a weighted symmetry, but the usefulness of that symmetry depends on the rest of the sextic and on the target density.

The displayed instance deliberately includes crossed terms rather than stopping at a sum of three disjoint binary forms. Terms such as an active quadratic times a polynomial involving another block test whether the relevant invariant is literal separation of variables or the weights of an adapted derivation. Those conditions are different: some mixed monomials have the correct weight, while others destroy it.

Fixing this monomial prevents the extension from being achieved merely by letting the target drift with every added coefficient.
