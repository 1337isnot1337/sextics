This higher-pole example replaces one Fermat pair by the binary cubic

p(u,w)=u^3+2\cdot u^2\cdot w-u\cdot w^2+w^3, \qquad (u,w)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2).

The other four sixth powers remain separate. It is a controlled step beyond the diagonal q=2 example, introducing one coupling while retaining a small coefficient problem.

The target is a two-term degree-twelve bundle,

A=X_0\cdot Y_0^{11}-\frac{610}{87}\cdot X_0\cdot Y_0^5\cdot Y_2^6.

The second monomial shifts six degrees into a spectator coordinate. This makes it possible to ask whether a lower-pole-style construction can be extended by a compatibility relation between the active cubic and a spectator sixth power.

The particular coefficient was predicted from a parameterized calculation, rather than selected after an unrestricted search for any solvable target. For a cubic u^3+\mu\cdot u^2w+\nu\cdot uw^2+\gamma\cdot w^3, the relevant expression involves its discriminant and 2\cdot\mu^3-9\cdot\mu\cdot\nu+27\cdot\gamma. The displayed fiber tests that relation at (2,-1,1), where the discriminant is nonzero. This is a useful example of geometry controlling a target coefficient.

The broader one-block theory in Chapter 2a covers all allowed targets, so the point here is an explicit, organized higher-pole presentation. A compatibility condition for one denominator or ansatz must not be mistaken for a restriction on every possible certificate. The instance asks for the full divisibility by F^3; a relation that works only modulo F, or only for the leading derivative equation, is insufficient. That makes it a substantive test of carrying the complete certificate mechanism to higher pole order.
