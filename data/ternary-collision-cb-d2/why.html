Write the active part of the sextic as a plane cubic p(u,w,x), with (u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2). The remaining coordinates contribute separate sixth powers. The special feature is an inflectional contact whose point lies on x=0, the coordinate line omitted from the target's odd factors.

The prescribed target has the form

A=X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot Q_2(u,w,x)

for a specified quadratic Q_2. This is a six-dimensional raw target character. The two derivative directions X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot p_u and X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot p_w already come from polynomial fields, so the interesting question concerns the remaining classes modulo those elementary directions. The remaining four-dimensional quotient is small enough to compare the images of different contact configurations explicitly.

The contact condition is stronger than ordinary tangency: the line meets the cubic three times at the same point. Its location on a branch coordinate is equally important. Pulling back a line under the squaring map changes its degree and interacts with the target parity. These features give a geometric reason to look for a rational primitive, while leaving the divergence cancellation to be established algebraically.

Together with the second-character example, this instance tests the scope of a configuration-based construction. It asks whether a criterion derived from successful tangent examples continues to work at a collision, and how the selected target depends on which coordinate contains that collision. The fiber is a controlled specialization of that geometry, not a claim that every tangent line on every ternary cubic serves the same class.
