The sextic differs from Fermat by the single coupling -2\cdot X_0^4\cdot Y_0^2. In squared coordinates this replaces two diagonal cubes by p(u,w)=u^3-2\cdot u^2\cdot w+w^3. It is a minimal-looking deformation, but the target

A=X_0\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1\cdot \left(X_0^2-\frac67\cdot Y_0^2\right)

places the odd factor in a spectator coordinate. This differs from an X-type target whose odd dependence lies inside the active pair.

The distinction matters for more than notation. A rational field must serve the prescribed parity character while cancelling the divergence caused by its pole. Changing the position of the odd exponent changes which spectator directions can participate and which coefficients are compatible. The small two-term target makes those constraints visible without requiring a dense sextic.

This example arose from comparing companion target directions on the same binary cubic. The coefficient -6/7 is one such direction; it is not an arbitrary adjustment to a separately solved monomial. Multiple independent directions on a fixed F are especially useful because certificates can be added after passing to a common denominator. That makes the target space, rather than the number of printed formulas, the meaningful measure of coverage.

The surrounding one-block family is covered by the binary-block constructions. The purpose of this entry is to study how that coverage can be realized economically over \mathbb Q, and how different presentations of the same class compare. The second rational fiber changes both the cubic and its companion coefficient, providing a direct check that the phenomenon is not peculiar to this one deformation.
