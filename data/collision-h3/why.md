The active cubic was chosen with a prescribed inflectional line. In \((u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2)\), it has the form
\[
p=(u-2\cdot w)^3+(u-2\cdot w+x)\cdot Q_2(u,w,x),
\]
where \(Q_2\) is the fixed quadratic displayed by the instance. On \(u-2\cdot w+x=0\), the restriction is exactly a cube. The resulting triple intersection is \((2:1:0)\), on the coordinate line \(x=0\).

This is a particularly clean test of whether contact geometry has predictive content. The fiber and its contact configuration were specified first; the target-sector support calculation was then made before a certificate was sought. Thus the relevant question was whether the geometric prediction could be converted into the full rational-field identity, rather than merely recognized in a successful denominator afterward.

The target lies in the character \(X_0\cdot Y_0\) times quadratic forms in \(u,w,x\). Its long coefficients specify one direction in that space, not an arbitrary request to serve every monomial on the cubic.

Comparing this example with [the ordinary tangent-and-residual fiber](#case/ternary-tangent-residual-h2-d2) isolates the effect of merging the residual intersection with the tangency point. Comparing it with [the other collision character](#case/ternary-collision-cbp-d2) tests the role of the coordinate line on which that merger occurs. These are controlled changes within a common mechanism. The example is valuable precisely because a concrete geometric configuration, its target character, and the algebraic certificate can be checked against one another. No low-degree pole is required by the problem statement; that is a property to explain if the configuration produces one.
