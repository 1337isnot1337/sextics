This target isolates the obstruction that distinguishes a block swap from a block sign. Put U=X+Y, V=X-Y. Then

F=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\bigl(f(U)+f(V)\bigr),\qquad \tau(U,V)=(V,U).

The equation is linearly split, but its involution exchanges the summands. The all-target block-sign theorem therefore does not apply. Chapter 3A, §§3A.4–3A.6 separates the supported joining and elliptic pieces from a remaining exterior square of the primitive cohomology of the K3 double plane w^2=f.

This particular target actually reaches that remaining sector. At q=1, the book's projector retains the two pure-block terms, with

B(U)=\frac{1}{64}\cdot A(U,U)=\frac{1}{64}\cdot U_0^2\cdot U_1^2\cdot U_2^2.

The projected numerator is B(U)-B(V). Unlike a target that projects to zero, this one really reaches the K3 summand. Modulo 5, the partials of f are fifth powers, so the exponents two in B show that its projection survives. Smooth Jacobian complete intersections have constant Hilbert function, carrying this conclusion to characteristic zero.

The geometric remainder is \Lambda^2 H^2(w^2=f)_{\mathrm{prim}}, with Hodge numbers (19,172,19) in degrees (3,1),(2,2),(1,3). Algebraic Kuga–Satake correspondences settle important special families, as discussed in §3A.6, but no such correspondence for this particular ternary sextic is supplied here. Its displayed equation is not in the special coordinate order-three normal form used there. We do not infer the absence of hidden symmetries or determine its Picard rank from that observation.

A promising contribution could identify additional geometry of this K3 surface, construct the required correspondence, or find a direct denominator bypassing that route. Solving this example would illuminate the part left over after the known elliptic mechanism, rather than repeat its supported sectors.

The Hessian symmetrizer space has dimension two, so the U/V splitting is the only nontrivial linear decomposition. A linear change cannot turn the block swap into a block-sign presentation. No general existence proof for this remaining class is asserted here.
