On the holdout sextic, set \(p=X_2\cdot Y_2\) and request
\[
A=p^3=X_2^3\cdot Y_2^3.
\]
This is the endpoint analogue of the central target \(X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\). The analogy is suggestive but does not come from a symmetry: the mixed terms form an oriented chain, so exchanging its ends changes the polynomial.

The endpoint does have a special simplification. Both its coupling \(X_1^3\cdot Y_1\cdot p\) and its target depend only on \(p\). If \(s=X_2^6+Y_2^6\), then
\[
(X_2^6-Y_2^6)^2=s^2-4\cdot p^6.
\]
This elementary relation explains why double-cover and elliptic descriptions arise when the other coordinates are treated as parameters. It offers a concrete geometric route to investigate without asserting that the varying family has already been solved.

The same symmetry also blocks the easiest argument. Exchanging \(X_2,Y_2\) fixes \(A\) and reverses the residue volume. The class consequently lies in the complementary sector to the reflection-supported one. More extensive finite-quotient calculations still leave it among the ten independent directions not covered inside the paired-sign-invariant target space. Its short expression in a quotient coordinate does not make it a known supported class.

This instance asks whether the pure endpoint cube can be handled more directly than targets involving the middle or initial pair. A successful relative calculation must account for how its primitives vary with those parameters and must ultimately produce the full six-variable certificate, or establish the protected existence statement by another route.
