Let K(z)=z_0^5\cdot z_1+z_1^5\cdot z_2+z_2^5\cdot z_0. The sextic F=K(X)+K(Y) is additively split, but neither block is a polynomial in coordinate squares. The cyclic order of its monomials gives useful symmetry while taking the example outside the most elementary reflection-based formulas.

The requested target is one cyclic quintic times a passive odd coordinate:

A=X_0^3\cdot X_1^2\cdot Y_0.

Compare the three active quintics U=X_0^3\cdot X_1^2, V=X_1^3\cdot X_2^2, and W=X_2^3\cdot X_0^2. Differences such as U-V occupy a two-dimensional zero-sum subspace. Serving those differences does not serve U individually; one more independent direction is required. This entry isolates exactly that issue.

The cyclic monomial map suggests Fourier coordinates over a field containing a cube root of unity. Such coordinates can simplify the equation but complicate the arithmetic and the Jacobian density. A rational answer must return from those algebraic coordinates without losing the prescribed monomial or introducing a divergence error. The target's apparently simple form therefore tests a substantial transport-and-descent problem.

The split theorem in Chapter 3A supplies broader geometric existence. The value of the particular instance is an explicit rational primitive for a single direction, together with a mechanism that can be compared against the Klein–Hesse difference target. It asks how much extra structure is needed to pass from differences to their constituents. Cyclic rotations and passive multipliers may then enlarge the useful target space, but that enlargement should follow from a verified construction, not from the symmetry of the equation alone.
