The \(Y\)-block of this sextic is
\[
Y_0^6+Y_1^6+3\cdot Y_2^6
-3\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1^2\cdot Y_2^2.
\]
In \(u=Y_0^2\), \(v=Y_1^2\), \(w=Y_2^2\), subtracting \(2\cdot w^3\) exposes the singular Hesse member \(u^3+v^3+w^3-3\cdot u\cdot v\cdot w\). Over a field containing cube roots of unity, that member is a triangle of lines. The full sextic still retains the additional sixth power and the separate \(X\)-block.

This distinction is important: a reducible member of an auxiliary pencil can be useful even when the hypersurface under study is smooth. Its components suggest candidate divisors or adapted directions; they do not imply that the challenge's \(F\) itself factors.

The target \(X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1^3\cdot Y_2\) combines a passive \(X\) factor with a nontrivial character in the Hesse block. The question is how the triangle geometry interacts with that particular class and whether its algebraic components can be organized into a rational presentation.

This example was used to compare a general three-quadric construction with more economical representations of the same fixed target. It is a useful elementary model for a larger theme: auxiliary factorizations can simplify a rational primitive without changing the variety or class being solved. [The trace-zero example](#case/family-trace-zero) replaces the triangle by a line times a conic, while [the fixed-target Hesse example](#case/fixed-witness-hesse-cofactor-d4-rational) mixes the quadratic coordinates. Those comparisons distinguish the role of factorization from the accident of a convenient coordinate system.
