A strict certificate from a binary-cubic Hessian construction

The certificate below serves the exact immutable instance

\begin{aligned} F={}&X_0^6+X_1^6+X_2^6+Y_0^6+Y_1^6+Y_2^6\\ &+X_0^3X_1Y_0Y_1+X_1^3X_2Y_0Y_1,\\ q={}&1,\qquad A=X_0^3Y_0^3. \end{aligned}

Its denominator has homogeneous degree 60, its six numerator components have homogeneous degree 61, and its quotient is zero. All coefficients belong to \mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\subset\mathbb C. The construction supplies an explicit strict rational vector field; no minimality of its denominator degree is claimed.

The main step is a rational coordinate map that turns one half of the target into a quadratic density on a cubic surface of the form f(u,p)+v^3+vz^2, where f is a binary cubic. A Hessian construction produces an explicit field for that density. Cofactor transport returns to the original six variables, and an exact sign symmetry supplies the other half. The rational coordinates are used to derive the formulas; the final certificate consists entirely of polynomials in the original variables.

1. Two targets related by a symmetry

Put

A_+=\frac{X_0^3(Y_0^3+Y_1^3)}2, \qquad A_-=\frac{X_0^3(Y_0^3-Y_1^3)}2.

Their sum is the prescribed A. The linear involution

\sigma(X_0,X_1,X_2,Y_0,Y_1,Y_2) =(X_0,-X_1,X_2,Y_0,-Y_1,Y_2)

preserves F and carries A_+ to A_-. Thus one explicit field for A_+, together with its transported field under \sigma, suffices.

This decomposition is compatible with the hidden reflections described by fringewidth in the existing instance discussion and recorded in why.md. The existence argument from those reflections was already known. The contribution here is the explicit polynomial certificate for the fixed target.

2. A rational map to a cubic surface

On the chart X_0Y_2\ne0, use the following ordered coordinates:

\Phi= \left( u=X_0^2,\quad p=Y_0Y_1,\quad v=Y_2^2,\quad z=\frac{Y_0^3-Y_1^3}{Y_2},\quad r=\frac{X_1}{X_0},\quad w=\frac{X_2}{X_0} \right).

Treat r,w as passive parameters and define

a=1+r^6+w^6,\qquad b=r+r^3w, \qquad f_0=au^3+bu^2p+2p^3.

The exact identity

Y_0^6+Y_1^6=(Y_0^3-Y_1^3)^2+2(Y_0Y_1)^3

gives

F=G_{\mathrm{cub}}\circ\Phi, \qquad G_{\mathrm{cub}}=f_0+v^3+vz^2.

The determinant in the displayed coordinate order is

\det(d\Phi)=\frac{12(Y_0^3+Y_1^3)}{X_0}.

Consequently the active density u^2/24 pulls back with the required Jacobian factor:

\det(d\Phi)\left(\frac{u^2}{24}\circ\Phi\right)=A_+.

For a rational active field \beta, append zero components in the two passive directions and set

V_+=\operatorname{adj}(d\Phi)\,(\beta\circ\Phi).

The rational Piola identity and the chain rule give

\beta(G_{\mathrm{cub}})=u^2/24,\quad \operatorname{div}\beta=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_+(F)=A_+,\quad\operatorname{div}V_+=0.

Derivatives of the passive coefficients are included in this full cofactor identity. They are not discarded when returning to the original variables. All four active coordinates have physical degree two and both passive coordinates have degree zero. Hence an active field of degree zero becomes a physical field of degree one, as required at q=1.

3. The active Hessian identity and the trace of Euler fields

Fix \lambda\in\mathbb C with \lambda^2=3. For the binary cubic f_0, define

\begin{aligned} \Delta&=324a^2+24b^3,\\ h_0&=-b^2u^2+18aup+6bp^2,\\ H&=(18au+12bp)\partial_u+(2b^2u-18ap)\partial_p,\\ k&=H(f_0)/3. \end{aligned}

The elementary binary-cubic identities used here are

H(h_0)=0,\qquad H(k)=3\Delta f_0,\qquad \Delta f_0^2-k^2=\frac49h_0^3,

and the target decomposition

\Delta u^2=108a\,\partial_u f_0+12b^2\,\partial_p f_0-12b h_0. \qquad\text{(1)}

These can all be checked by polynomial differentiation and expansion.

To explain their origin, work temporarily over the generic parameter field, where \Delta\ne0, and adjoin a square root of \Delta. The matrix of H on linear forms squares to \Delta I. In its two eigen-coordinates the cubic becomes a sum of cubes, with cubic summands

f_\pm=\frac12\left(f_0\pm\frac{k}{\sqrt\Delta}\right).

The other binary cubic has the explicit decomposition

v^3+vz^2 =\frac12\left(v+\frac{\lambda z}{3}\right)^3 +\frac12\left(v-\frac{\lambda z}{3}\right)^3.

Write r_0=v+\lambda z/3 and g=r_0^3/2. The Euler field on the coordinate belonging to f_+, together with the Euler field on r_0, has divergence two. It acts on h_0 with eigenvalue one and on f_++g with eigenvalue three. Therefore

\frac{h_0(E_++E_{r_0})}{3(f_++g)}

has zero divergence and sends G_{\mathrm{cub}} to h_0. The conjugate expression with f_- has the same target. Average these rational fields, with their denominators included. Using the Hessian identity above removes \sqrt\Delta and gives

\begin{aligned} d_0&=9\Delta g^2+9\Delta f_0g+h_0^3,\\ J&=\Delta(2g+f_0)(u,p,r_0,\lambda r_0)-k(H_u,H_p,0,0),\\ \beta_h&=\frac{3h_0J}{4d_0}. \end{aligned}

Thus \beta_h(G_{\mathrm{cub}})=h_0 and \operatorname{div}\beta_h=0. Combining it with the two constant coordinate fields from (1) gives the desired density:

\beta= \frac{(36ad_0,4b^2d_0,0,0)-3bh_0J}{8\Delta d_0}, \qquad \beta(G_{\mathrm{cub}})=\frac{u^2}{24}, \quad\operatorname{div}\beta=0. \qquad\text{(2)}

Every component of the numerator in (2) is divisible by \Delta. For example, the two nontrivial cancellations follow from

\begin{aligned} 36ah_0^2+3bkH_u &=\Delta u(3abu^3+36ap^2u+b^2pu^2+18bp^3),\\ 4b^2h_0^2+3bkH_p &=\frac{\Delta b}{3}(-9apu^3+b^2u^4+3bp^2u^2+18p^4). \end{aligned}

Canceling this common factor before physical denominator clearing is what leads to degree 30 for a half-target. The formulas below contain neither \sqrt\Delta nor a discriminant denominator. Only the fixed constant \lambda remains. The derivation on a dense chart explains the construction; the resulting cleared polynomial identities hold without any chart or discriminant restriction.

4. The explicit half-target polynomials

The following formulas use only the original variables. Define

\begin{aligned} p&=Y_0Y_1,&s&=Y_0^3-Y_1^3,&S&=Y_0^3+Y_1^3,&T&=Y_2^3,\\ G_0&=X_0^6+X_1^6+X_2^6,&c&=X_0^3X_1+X_1^3X_2,\\ \delta&=324G_0^2+24c^3,&h&=-c^2+18G_0p+6cp^2,\\ f&=G_0+cp+2p^3,&R&=T+\lambda s/3,\\ W&=R^3+fT,&Z&=R^3(R^3+2fT). \end{aligned}

In particular, F=f+s^2+Y_2^6 is exactly the original sextic. Set

\begin{aligned} D_+&=9\delta Z+4h^3T^2,\\ p_0&=G_0c+12G_0p^2+c^2p/3+6cp^3,\\ p_1&=-3G_0p+c^2/3+cp^2+6p^4,\\ U&=81G_0Z-3chWT+3hp_0T^2,\\ V&=9c^2Z-3chWpT+chp_1T^2,\\ B&=-3chWR. \end{aligned}

The six numerators, in the required variable order, are

N_+=X_0^3\left( \begin{array}{c} 3SX_0U\\ 3SX_1U\\ 3SX_2U\\ 6Y_1^2V+Y_0(s+2\lambda T)B\\ 6Y_0^2V-Y_1(s+2\lambda T)B\\ 3SY_2B \end{array} \right). \qquad\text{(3)}

These are the cofactor transport of (2), after canceling the common discriminant factor and clearing all coordinate denominators. In particular, no extra factor of X_0 or Y_2 is needed in D_+. The two exact identities are

N_+(F)=A_+D_+, \qquad D_+\operatorname{div}N_+-N_+(D_+)=0. \qquad\text{(4)}

Here N(P)=\sum_iN_i\partial_iP. Formula (4) can also be verified directly in the polynomial ring over \mathbb Q(\lambda), using only \lambda^2=3. This direct check is independent of the coordinate-map and diagonalization explanations.

5. Recovering the exact fixed target

Let \sigma P=P\circ\sigma on polynomials and put

D_-=\sigma D_+, \qquad (N_-)_i=\epsilon_i\,\sigma((N_+)_i), \qquad \epsilon=(1,-1,1,1,-1,1).

The component signs are necessary: this is transport of a vector field, not just substitution in six unrelated polynomials. Since \sigma F=F, the chain rule and invariance of divergence imply

N_-(F)=A_-D_-, \qquad D_-\operatorname{div}N_--N_-(D_-)=0.

The final certificate is

\boxed{ D=D_+D_-,\qquad N=D_-N_++D_+N_-,\qquad Q=0. } \qquad\text{(5)}

It represents the sum of the two strict fields. In particular,

N(F)=X_0^3Y_0^3D, \qquad D\operatorname{div}N-N(D)=0.

Consequently the precise cleared expression in the verifier is

D(AD-N(F))+F\bigl(D\operatorname{div}N-N(D)\bigr) =0=F^2Q.

Thus (5) serves the exact statement with the original (F,q,A).

Under the global involution \tau:(X,Y)\mapsto(X,-Y), the polynomials s,S,T,R are odd. All of p,G_0,c,\delta,h,f,Z,p_0,p_1,U,V,B are even, while W is odd. Formula (3) therefore makes D_+ even, the first three numerator components odd, and the last three even. The involutions \sigma and \tau commute, so (5) has precisely the same parity pattern. This symmetry is an additional property of the construction; the unrestricted certificate contract imposes no field-parity restriction.

Relative primality has a short explicit check. Regard F,D_+ as polynomials in t=Y_2, with coefficients in the other five variables. The leading coefficient of F is one. Specialize

X_0=X_1=X_2=Y_0=Y_1=1.

Then F becomes t^6+7, and D_+ becomes

d(t)=27972t^{18}+391608t^{12}+953312t^6.

They satisfy the exact Euclidean identity

d(t)=(t^6+7)(27972t^{12}+195804t^6-417316)+2921212. \qquad\text{(6)}

The nonzero constant remainder gives a Bezout certificate over \mathbb C. The repository's relPrime_of_specialization lemma lifts this to \operatorname{IsRelPrime}(F,D_+), using the unit leading coefficient of F. This proves relative primality, rather than merely checking that F does not divide D_+. The same specialization proves D_+\ne0.

The automorphism \sigma preserves F, so relative primality and nonvanishing also hold for D_-. Relative primality is preserved under products, and the polynomial ring over \mathbb C is a domain. Hence D=D_+D_-\ne0 and \operatorname{IsRelPrime}(F,D), completing the denominator conditions.

7. Relation to the formal certificate

The main polynomial calculation can be organized in only five independent variables G_0,c,p,R,T, over \mathbb Q. Define the derivations

\mathcal E=6G_0\partial_{G_0}+4c\partial_c, \qquad \mathcal K=(3R+12T)\partial_R+9T\partial_T

and the polynomial

M=3\mathcal E(U)+18U+6\partial_pV+\mathcal K(B)+6B.

The two identities in that smaller polynomial ring are

\begin{aligned} D_+&=6U(6G_0+4cp)+12V(c+6p^2)+36BR^2,\\ D_+M&=3U\mathcal E(D_+)+6V\partial_pD_++B\mathcal K(D_+). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(7)}

After substitution, direct differentiation of (3) gives

\operatorname{div}N_+=X_0^3S M

and

N_+(D_+)=X_0^3S \left(3U\mathcal E(D_+)+6V\partial_pD_++B\mathcal K(D_+)\right).

Thus (7) supplies the two strict half-target identities. The contribution 18U in M includes differentiation of the common factor X_0^3 in (3). The polynomials D_+,U,V,B,M have respectively 18, 12, 11, 10, and 24 terms when expanded in these five variables. This keeps the algebraic core of the verification separate from the much larger expansion in the six original coordinates.

The displayed formulas specify all witness data: denominator degree 60, the polynomial D, the six entries of N, and Q=0. For this data, the five fields of HodgeComputer.Verify require D\ne0, homogeneity of D of degree 60, homogeneity of every N_i of degree 60+6q-5=61, \operatorname{IsRelPrime}(F,D) over \mathbb C, and the exact cleared identity with right-hand side F^{q+1}Q=0. Equations (3)–(6) isolate those algebraic and side-condition obligations.
