The target on the central holdout is

A=X_1\cdot Y_0^4\cdot Y_1.

It uses the first two coordinate pairs and omits the endpoint pair entirely. Compared with the central cubic X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3, it transfers odd-coordinate degree toward Y_0 while bringing both middle coordinates into the class. This tests whether the geometry of the first coupling can be used without controlling a balanced cubic at the chain's beginning.

There is a tempting local interpretation: Y_0^4 resembles a coefficient that could multiply a construction in X_1,Y_1. But the first mixed term is X_0^3\cdot X_1\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1, so Y_0 is not separate from that active pair. The second term couples the same middle coordinates to X_2,Y_2. Treating either side as an arbitrary passive polynomial would lose part of the derivative equation.

The monomial is invariant under each paired sign change and under exchanging X_2,Y_2. The latter does not give the needed support: the residue acquires the sign of the volume form. More comprehensive quotient calculations place this target among ten independent directions outside the known 48-dimensional support inside the 58-dimensional paired-sign sector. Thus its simpler variable support does not make it redundant with an already covered target.

A useful approach would explain the whole character space containing this monomial, rather than choosing an unrelated numerator after finding a convenient denominator. The nearby mixed endpoint target trades two powers of Y_0 for an endpoint product and another X_1. Comparing their behavior can reveal whether a proposed support sees exponent distribution or only the shared symmetry.
