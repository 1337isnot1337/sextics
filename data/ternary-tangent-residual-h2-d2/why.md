This entry replaces a binary cubic in squared coordinates by a ternary one. Write
\[
F=p(u,w,x)+X_2^6+Y_1^6+Y_2^6,
\qquad (u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2).
\]
The active geometry is now a plane cubic, so its lines and their intersection multiplicities become potential sources of information about the six-variable certificate problem.

The selected cubic has a distinguished line \(\ell=u-2\cdot w+x\). On the parametrization \((u,w,x)=(s+2\cdot r,s+r,s)\), its restriction is \(-\frac52\cdot s\cdot r^2\). Thus the line is tangent at \((1:1:1)\), with a separate residual intersection at \((2:1:0)\). The latter lies on \(x=0\), the branch coordinate associated with the even active variable in this target character.

That placement is the substantive feature. Tangency alone gives a repeated intersection, but it does not establish that a divisor supports the desired cohomology class. The square-coordinate map introduces ramification, and the location of the residual point relative to that ramification can decide which target directions are accessible. Here the target belongs to \(X_0\cdot Y_0\) times the quadratic forms in \(u,w,x\), rather than an unrestricted sextic space.

The fiber was deliberately chosen to test this tangent-and-residual configuration. It provides a controlled comparison with [the inflectional collision](#case/collision-h3), where the tangent and residual intersections merge. The relevant question is whether the plane geometry predicts an actual rational primitive with the correct target character. [Chapter 2](#technical-information/explicit-constructions) explains the contact and transport framework; the construction popup gives the instance-specific algebra. A small polar degree is an outcome to investigate, not an extra condition in the challenge.
