The active block belongs to the Hesse pencil
\[
p_t(u,w,x)=u^3+w^3+x^3+t\cdot u\cdot w\cdot x,
\qquad (u,w,x)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2,X_1^2),
\]
and this entry takes \(t=-2\). The target is the individual monomial \(X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\). Compared with [the compatible bundle at \(t=1\)](#case/f3-block-x0-quartic-strict), this changes both the parameter and the question of whether companion monomials can be avoided.

The plane cubic has several tangent lines with residual intersection at the same distinguished point. Their parameters satisfy a cubic equation, so a rational fiber need not have every useful contact line defined over \(\mathbb Q\). The associated contact conics offer a second geometric description of those same algebraic choices. This makes the example a concrete test of whether several contact contributions can be assembled into one rational answer for a fixed target.

The distinction between a contact and its target image is essential. Finding three lines or conics does not prove that their contributions span the requested monomial. The coefficients of a trace must be chosen to reach that particular direction, and the resulting rational field must still satisfy the divergence condition. These are finite algebraic questions that can be investigated without treating the six-variable equation as a completely unstructured search.

The Hesse-web family emerged from comparing successful tangent and conic descriptions, including the coefficient fields they shared. This fiber retains that mathematical question in a compact setting. The unequal spectator sixth powers help distinguish the active contact mechanism from an accidental symmetry of Fermat.
